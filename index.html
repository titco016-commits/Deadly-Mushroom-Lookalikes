Mushrooms are nature's most abundant source of food on the planet. Mycelium networking systems of over 14,000 different types of fungi cover the entire planet. The abundance of this nutrient-packed fungi is hindered by the presence of species that can easily kill or seriously impair a human whose hubris got the best of them.

Minnesota is home to thousands of those mushroom species. The abundance of lakes and moist areas makes Minnesota a prime breeding ground for all kinds of fungus. Deep wooded valleys, downed trees, oak trunks, pine needles, maple leaves and boggy shorelines are all places a forager may know to look.

This web page is meant to help inform the average person of the fact that these dangerous lookalikes to commonly eaten wild mushrooms in Minnesota exist, NOT to help someone identify a mushroom for consumption.
